Overview

Six ideas, one motion

Every kinematics problem is built from these six building blocks.

🏃

Motion

A change in an object's position with respect to time and a reference point.

📏

Distance

Total path length covered by an object — always positive, a scalar quantity.

📍

Displacement

Shortest straight-line change in position, with direction — a vector quantity.

⚡

Speed

Rate of change of distance travelled per unit time — how fast, no direction.

🧭

Velocity

Rate of change of displacement per unit time — speed with a direction attached.

🚀

Acceleration

Rate of change of velocity per unit time — how quickly velocity itself changes.

Study Notes

Concepts, explained simply

Every idea with a plain definition, a worked example, and a quick visual cue.

🏃

Motion

An object is in motion if its position changes continuously with respect to a fixed reference point over time.

Example: A bus leaving a bus stop is in motion relative to the stop.
🧍

Rest

An object is at rest if its position does not change with respect to a fixed reference point over time.

Example: A parked car is at rest relative to the road.
📏

Distance

The total length of the actual path travelled by an object, regardless of direction. It is a scalar.

Example: Walking 3 m north then 3 m south covers a distance of 6 m.
📍

Displacement

The shortest straight-line distance between the initial and final position, along with direction. It is a vector.

Example: Walking 3 m north then 3 m south gives a displacement of 0 m.
⚖️

Scalar vs Vector

Scalars have only magnitude (distance, speed). Vectors have magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity).

Example: "5 m/s" is a speed; "5 m/s east" is a velocity.
⚡

Speed

Distance travelled per unit time. Speed = Distance ÷ Time. It tells us how fast, but not which way.

Example: Covering 100 m in 20 s gives a speed of 5 m/s.
🧭

Velocity

Displacement per unit time. Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time. It includes direction of motion.

Example: 5 m/s towards the east is a velocity.
🚀

Acceleration

The rate at which velocity changes with time. Acceleration = (Final − Initial Velocity) ÷ Time.

Example: Going from 0 to 20 m/s in 4 s gives 5 m/s².
➡️

Uniform Motion

An object covers equal displacements in equal intervals of time — velocity stays constant.

Example: A train moving at a constant 60 km/h on a straight track.
📈

Uniform Acceleration

An object's velocity changes by equal amounts in equal intervals of time.

Example: A ball in free fall gains about 9.8 m/s of speed every second.
Reference

The five equations you need

Everything in this chapter reduces to these formulas.

Equation of Motion
v = u + at

Final velocity equals initial velocity plus the product of acceleration and time.

Equation of Motion
s = ut + ½at²

Displacement equals initial velocity times time, plus half of acceleration times time squared.

Equation of Motion
v² = u² + 2as

Final velocity squared equals initial velocity squared plus twice the product of acceleration and displacement.

Average Velocity
v̄ = (u + v) / 2

For uniform acceleration, average velocity is the mean of initial and final velocity.

Average Speed
Total Distance / Total Time

The overall rate of covering distance across the whole journey, ignoring direction.
